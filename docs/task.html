Before We Begin

Here's a quick walkthrough of how this study works.

What you'll do

You'll read 3 transcripts of spoken conversations. For each transcript, you'll answer a set of survey questions about it.

How to answer questions

1

Read the question on the right side of the screen.

2

Highlight the relevant text in the transcript on the left that helped you answer. Select text by clicking and dragging over it.

3

Select your answer from the options. If there isn't enough information in the transcript, select "Not enough information to answer this question" — no highlight is needed in that case.

4

Click Next to move to the next question. You can go back to change your answers.

Example

If the question asks "What did the witness see the blue car do?", you would highlight the relevant part like this:

I was driving to work when I saw a blue sedan speed through the intersection without stopping at the red light. Then there was a loud crash.

To remove a highlight, just click on it.

Note: In your condition, you'll see four different versions of each transcript (Verbatim, Non-Verbatim, Enhanced, Bullet Points). You can switch between them using the labels above the transcript. Feel free to highlight across multiple versions.

About This Study

In this study, we investigated how different transcription styles affect people's ability to understand and evaluate spoken conversations. You were randomly assigned to one of five conditions: Verbatim, Non-Verbatim, Enhanced, Bullet Points, or an interactive tool (SpeechSpectrum) that lets you switch between all four.

We are interested in whether the way a transcript is formatted changes how people perceive its clarity, accuracy, and professionalism — and whether having access to multiple formats (via SpeechSpectrum) helps people answer evaluation questions more effectively.

Your highlights help us understand which parts of the transcript people rely on when forming their judgments, and how that differs across transcription styles.

If you have any questions about this research, please contact the research team through Prolific.
