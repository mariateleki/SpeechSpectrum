<p>- Mr. Bates was questioned about why his offenses were directed against women.<br>
- He explained that his mother left, struggled with heroin addiction, engaged in prostitution, and later died of AIDS; he said witnessing her life shaped feelings he later had to confront and overcome.<br>
- Bates said he visited his mother in a nursing home before her death, told her how her absence and choices affected him, and felt he had forgiven her, gaining deeper respect and love for her.<br>
- The seriousness of his past assaults was emphasized, including an incident where he punched a woman multiple times in the back of the head; Bates acknowledged the concern.<br>
- Bates described positive changes in his life, including marriage, stepchildren, a daughter from a prior marriage, involvement with the Masons, and work as a barber.<br>
- He now works privately after being robbed at gunpoint in his shop but continues to train aspiring barbers and remains willing to help others.<br>
- The chair connected Bates’s mother’s struggles to trauma often experienced by victimized women and asked him to consider the impact of his sexual assault on the young woman he harmed.<br>
- Bates acknowledged the assault must have been devastating and apologized.<br>
- The chair noted that Bates’s application showed unusual candor in accepting responsibility, including admitting the act was against the victim’s will and that she tried to stop him.<br>
- The chair emphasized the victim likely experienced lasting trauma, fear, anxiety, and loss of safety, and urged Bates to deepen his understanding of that harm.<br>
- No objections were raised regarding Mr. Bates.</p>
