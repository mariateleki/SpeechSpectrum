<p>Mr. Bates was questioned about why his offenses had been directed against women, and he explained that he was raised by his father after his mother left, struggled with heroin addiction, and engaged in prostitution; he said witnessing her life in the community shaped feelings he later had to confront and overcome. He stated that his mother died of AIDS, but before her death he visited her in a nursing home, told her how her absence and choices had affected him, and felt that he had long since forgiven her, gaining deeper respect and love for her during that conversation. The seriousness of his past assaults was emphasized, including an incident in which he punched a woman in the back of the head multiple times, and Bates acknowledged the concern. He also described positive changes in his life, including marriage, stepchildren, a daughter from a prior marriage, involvement with the Masons, and work as a barber; although he now works privately after being robbed at gunpoint in his shop, he said he continues to train aspiring barbers and remains open to helping others. The chair then connected Bates’s mother’s struggles with the trauma often experienced by women who survive victimization and asked him to consider the impact of his sexual assault on the young woman he harmed. Bates acknowledged that it must have been devastating and apologized, while the chair noted that his application showed unusual candor in accepting responsibility, including admitting that the act was against the victim’s will and that she tried to stop him. The chair further explained that the victim likely experienced lasting trauma, fear, anxiety, and a loss of safety, especially because she had been visiting friends and expected to be secure, and urged Bates to deepen his understanding of that harm in light of his own family experiences and the daughters he has raised. No objections were raised regarding Mr. Bates.</p>
