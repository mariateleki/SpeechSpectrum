<p>- Lorenze said he has not spoken to the victim, his sister, in 30 years and has respected her wishes by not initiating contact or forcing an apology.<br>
- He acknowledged that accepting an apology should not be her responsibility and that any interaction must occur only on her terms.<br>
- Lorenze recognized the profound harm his actions may have caused, including lasting effects on trust, emotional security, and mental health.<br>
- The panel noted the importance of his understanding that forgiveness is not something he is owed.<br>
- Lorenze said he is tentatively interested in pursuing law school, believing his experience with the legal system and empathy for those involved could be valuable.<br>
- The Office of Victim Services said the victim wished to participate, but her statement had not been sent directly to the office; it had apparently been routed through family members and given to Lorenze’s attorney.<br>
- The panel allowed the attorney to read the statement after relying on her representation that it was authentic.<br>
- In the statement, the victim said she had not seen or spoken to Lorenze in 30 years but had carefully considered the matter, including speaking with their mother.<br>
- The victim concluded that a pardon would not pose a danger to herself or others.<br>
- She emphasized that her recommendation was not based on family reconciliation or idealized forgiveness, but on Lorenze’s current functioning and the role of decades of legal scrutiny in preventing further harm.<br>
- The victim personally recommended the pardon.<br>
- The chair described the statement as an act of grace, and Lorenze said he understood and appreciated the limits and conditions under which it was offered.<br>
- The panel members raised no objections.</p>
