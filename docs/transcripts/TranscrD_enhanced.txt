<p>At Mr. Feliciano’s pardon hearing, the Chair questioned him closely about his history of alcohol-related domestic violence, including a 2019 conviction stemming from an incident in which he was intoxicated, threatened the victim with a bat and knife, and left the scene, as well as prior acts involving physical aggression, strangulation, threats, and striking a coworker with a motor vehicle. The Chair expressed concern that although Feliciano described his offenses as mistakes and linked them to alcohol, he did not appear to have a structured sobriety practice or a clear understanding of the roots of his alcohol abuse. Feliciano responded that he grew up witnessing his father drink heavily and abuse his mother, and while he did not want to blame others, he believed those experiences may have contributed to his own behavior; the Chair noted that he had tragically repeated the violence he had witnessed and emphasized concern for his children, who are now 16 and seven. A victim statement was then read in support of his pardon, saying Feliciano had remained alcohol-free since his last conviction, taken responsibility, shown remorse, become a licensed plumber, become involved in his sons’ activities, and worked to rebuild his life, while his criminal record continued to limit his career advancement. Board member Cizauskas acknowledged the victim’s support and Feliciano’s apparent progress but opposed the pardon, citing the seriousness and repetition of the domestic violence cases, four unsatisfactory probation terms, and the fact that only about six years had passed since his last conviction despite a prior nine-year arrest-free period. The Chair agreed with that concern. Board member Palmieri, however, supported granting the pardon, giving significant weight to the victim’s statement and noting Feliciano’s sobriety since 2018, stable plumbing career, family support, involvement with his children, completion of programming, and evidence of rehabilitation.</p>
