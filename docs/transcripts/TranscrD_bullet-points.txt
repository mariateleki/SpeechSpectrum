<p>- At Mr. Feliciano’s pardon hearing, the Chair questioned him about his history of alcohol-related domestic violence, including a 2019 conviction involving intoxication, threats with a bat and knife, and leaving the scene.<br>
- The Chair also referenced prior acts involving physical aggression, strangulation, threats, and striking a coworker with a motor vehicle.<br>
- The Chair expressed concern that Feliciano described his offenses as mistakes tied to alcohol but lacked a structured sobriety practice or clear understanding of the roots of his alcohol abuse.<br>
- Feliciano said he grew up witnessing his father drink heavily and abuse his mother, and believed those experiences may have contributed to his behavior, though he did not want to blame others.<br>
- The Chair noted that Feliciano had repeated the violence he witnessed and emphasized concern for his children, ages 16 and seven.<br>
- A victim statement supported the pardon, stating that Feliciano had remained alcohol-free since his last conviction, taken responsibility, shown remorse, become a licensed plumber, engaged in his sons’ activities, and worked to rebuild his life.<br>
- The victim statement also said Feliciano’s criminal record continued to limit his career advancement.<br>
- Board member Cizauskas opposed the pardon despite acknowledging the victim’s support and Feliciano’s progress, citing repeated serious domestic violence, four unsatisfactory probation terms, and only about six years since his last conviction.<br>
- The Chair agreed with Cizauskas’s concern.<br>
- Board member Palmieri supported granting the pardon, emphasizing the victim’s statement, Feliciano’s sobriety since 2018, stable plumbing career, family support, involvement with his children, completion of programming, and evidence of rehabilitation.</p>
